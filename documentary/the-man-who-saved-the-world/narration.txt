September, 1983. The Cold War is at its most dangerous. Thousands of nuclear missiles stand ready to fly.
Just after midnight, in a secret bunker outside Moscow, Lieutenant Colonel Stanislav Petrov is on duty.
Then, the alarm. Satellites report an American missile launch. Then another. Five in all.
Protocol says: report it. A Soviet counterstrike could follow within minutes.
But Petrov doubts it. A real first strike would mean hundreds of missiles, not five. Ground radar confirms nothing.
He calls it a false alarm.
He was right. Sunlight on high clouds had fooled the satellites.
Petrov received no medal. His story stayed secret for fifteen years. The man who saved the world.
